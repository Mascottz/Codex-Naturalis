Margin notes

The duel wall

Each quarrel stays close to its mathematics, with the ending stated as cleanly as the record allows.

01 / The cubic

Tartaglia against Cardano

x³ + px = q

Tartaglia guarded a cubic solution as a sworn secret and shared it with Cardano under a promise of silence. Cardano printed the method in Ars Magna after learning that del Ferro had found it earlier. The oath was broken in print, and the credit is shared among the discoverers and the algebra that carried the result.

02 / The quickest descent

The Bernoulli brothers

x = a(t − sin t), y = a(1 − cos t)

Johann Bernoulli posted the brachistochrone as a public challenge, asking for the curve of quickest descent under gravity. Both Bernoulli brothers answered, and Newton is said to have solved it overnight after the challenge reached him. The winning curve is a cycloid, while the overnight story survives as a reported episode rather than a timed record.

03 / Two notations

Newton against Leibniz

dy/dx versus fluxions

Newton developed fluxions and Leibniz published the notation dy/dx, with independent calculus programs taking shape in the seventeenth century. A priority dispute turned their followers into rival camps and burdened British mathematics for decades. The modern record recognizes both as independent founders, while the dispute never produced a single uncontested victor.

04 / The quartic

Ferrari against del Ferro's heirs

x⁴ → x³ resolvent

Ferrari's quartic method reduces a general fourth-degree equation to a cubic resolvent. The surviving letters document Ferrari's contest with Tartaglia, while del Ferro's heirs belong to the earlier guarded story Cardano inherited. The record does not show a direct Ferrari-versus-del-Ferro-heirs duel; the solution entered print in Cardano's Ars Magna.
